How it works

1 · Where am I? (GPS)

Your phone hears signals from satellites. Each signal says how far away that satellite is, so the phone sits somewhere on a circle around it. Three circles meet at one point: that's you. Drag the satellites.

2 · Phones send their speed

Phones with location turned on send anonymous pings: which road, and how fast. Here the pings are fake. A jam sends slow pings. The 99-phone wagon sends 99 pings at walking pace, like Simon Weckert's 2020 stunt in Berlin.

3 · The live speed of each road

Average all the pings on a road. A few fast cars can't beat 99 slow phones, so the wagon makes an empty road look jammed.

4 · Green, orange, red

Compare the live speed with the speed the road has when it's empty. Under 75% turns orange. Under 40% turns red.

5 · Predicting your arrival time

A jam now may be gone when you get there. The prediction slides today's live speed back to the usual speed as the minutes pass. Move the fade slider and watch the fastest route change.

6 · The best route

Dijkstra's algorithm grows outward from the start, always taking the cheapest junction next. It runs twice: once with kilometres as the cost (shortest), once with predicted minutes (fastest). Plain Dijkstra is fine for 2 km. Real engines use Contraction Hierarchies or multi-level techniques to search whole continents in milliseconds.

Not a navigation app. Traffic is simulated. The page never asks for your location.
